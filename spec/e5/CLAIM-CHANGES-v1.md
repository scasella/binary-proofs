# E5 normal-entry refinement: claims and interpretation

Experiment `BP-20261001-E5-NORMAL-V1` is additive. It preserves the accepted E2/E3 specifications, model files, proofs, receipts and delivered package. It does not retrospectively reinterpret their unrestricted raw-RFLAGS domains as physical execution domains. Human specification and architectural review remain pending.

The E5 formal premise adds exactly `~bit 16 (read rflags s) /\ ~bit 8 (read rflags s)` to the existing public precondition. These are RF=0 and TF=0. Its instruction relation adds RF clearing to the existing qualified scalar transition. The checked-run obligation must establish that every original successor preserves these two zero bits and that the clearing relation and original relation therefore coincide throughout each admitted execution. An existence-only correspondence is insufficient. The product-state refinement retains the previously declared omitted-state semantics; it does not independently certify those architectural definitions.

The frozen targets cover both OpenSSL routines and the actual composed `secret_equal_and_clear` caller. Their functional results, permitted accesses, finite actual return, stack restoration, protected return slot, preserved ABI state, input-overlap domain, lengths and exact production artifacts are unchanged. E5 carries the same posts and strengthened normal-state property through its own transition relation. The Crypto post retains the public architectural observation trace; the additional relational trace goal is explicitly a normal-domain restriction of the original trace theorem. No caller trace theorem is added. No new production instruction bytes are discovered by this refinement.

The existing caller already proves canonicality of its two resolved GOT values and their preservation along both actual PLT paths. E5 reuses that claim. The fixed post-initialization binding remains a loader/environment premise. Neither the original generic indirect-transfer supplier nor E5 certifies arbitrary noncanonical resolved targets.

The physical interpretation additionally requires a legal initial architectural state, Linux user long mode and ordinary accessible mappings, disabled CET under the frozen profile, no enabled ordinary instruction/data debug breakpoints, and no delivered external/debug event or debug-agent modification during the scoped call. These conditions are enumerated in [PHYSICAL-DOMAIN-v1.md](../../development/approved-plan/normal-entry-e5-v1/PHYSICAL-DOMAIN-v1.md); they are architectural interpretation assumptions, not an opaque formal `valid_state` premise or an assumed functional-correctness invariant. Reserved RFLAGS bit 1 must be one. The old caller witness with the raw word zero is a logical non-vacuity witness, not a physically legal flags image. Legal flag values do not by themselves supply a feasible Linux loaded-image witness.

The specific RF housekeeping finding can be classified `CLOSED_WITH_NORMAL_ENTRY_BOUND` only after the frozen all-successor correspondence and all three interface refinements pass fresh checking. RF=1/debug/resume entries and general exception/fault-priority correspondence remain `EXCLUDED_SCOPE`. Unqualified full-processor correspondence remains `NOT_ESTABLISHED`; that does not reopen the specifically closed RF=0/TF=0 result. The old review's `HIGH_OPEN` finding remains part of immutable history. Current classification belongs in the generated status registry, not in overwritten historical receipts.

Intel Volume 3B 253669-078US, section 18.3.1.1, describes RF clearing at instruction start after its listed initial checks; the delivered later Volume 3A describes clearing after successful execution. This difference is harmless only within an established invariant-zero domain: clearing zero is identity at either placement. It is not justification for an RF=1 correspondence theorem. Section 18.3.1.4 supplies the TF/single-step boundary. These precise conditions replace an undifferentiated warning about hardware correspondence. The independent agent's primary-reference review is recorded separately from formal proof acceptance.

The expected E5 literals and definitions were frozen before main search. The additional RF=1 nonidentity target is a mathematical property of the clearing definition; it is not a checked physical execution counterexample. Any changed formal meaning requires a new experiment and preservation of the previous outcome. Fresh acceptance must reconstruct the full accepted callee/caller dependency chain and audit live theorem objects, expanded definitions, unchanged axioms, exact bytes, loaded images and immutable sources; a warm search result or copied receipt is insufficient.
