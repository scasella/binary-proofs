## Specific refinements requiring review

The production encoding `F3 C3` is treated as the existing near `RET` under the separately versioned REP-RET decoder extension. The retained primary AMD documentation, pinned architectural references and limited emulated diagnostics justify the stated interpretation. They do not prove a physical ISA semantics correspondence theorem. Do not merge that review with the proof that this model returns safely.

E3 repairs the actual near-indirect PLT transfer's operand width, target interpretation and GOT-load observation, and requalifies the supplier interfaces. Actual caller PLT/GOT transitions use the fixed canonical constants `0x10241840` and `0x102417d0` and prove required target/byte preservation under their access/frame conditions. Generic arbitrary-target facts do not certify noncanonical targets. Thus the canonical-GOT status is concretely resolved for these images, with loader-created binding still assumed; it is not an open blanket dispatch assumption.

E5 adds `~bit 16(read rflags s) /\ ~bit 8(read rflags s)` and models RF clearing after a qualified transition. Because every admitted original successor preserves RF=TF=0, clearing is identity throughout the admitted run. Different architectural descriptions of clearing at instruction start versus successful retirement coincide for this invariant-zero domain. This does not justify RF=1 behavior, faults or debug-resume correspondence. The definitions and expanded conclusions are in `contracts-and-rules-v1.json`; the architectural boundary is enumerated in `normal-entry-e5-v1/PHYSICAL-DOMAIN-v1.md` and `spec/e5/CLAIM-CHANGES-v1.md`.

Physical initial state has additional legality requirements: reserved/fixed RFLAGS bits, legal x87/SIMD/MXCSR and segment/control state. Those are interpretation conditions, not hidden target invariants. Canonical-top abstract witnesses and mathematically admissible flag records do not demonstrate feasible Linux process mappings or unrestricted physical state. The selected scalar routines preserve existing SIMD/opmask and the declared added fields under the qualified model.

The C3 fixed MOV site is **proof-level qualification within the existing predicate**, not a semantic extension. Its exact site/application and relation to extension clauses 3/4 are exposed in `REVIEW-GUIDE-v1.md`. The human reviewer must assess both architecture and extension-policy classification; a checked proof in a definition does not independently validate that definition or policy interpretation.

## Final artifact and linkage scope

The unchanged Debian production libcrypto is SHA-256 `76dd3d93e5ee48950a92a58d59b94de8143847f91a80d9682c938767b991577d`, at fixed supplier bias `0x10000000`. The public supplier entries are `0x10241840` and `0x102417d0`. C1/C2/C3 caller ELFs and concrete biases are separately pinned in the release index and their original inventories. Their actual immediate-relative CALLs and six-byte RIP-relative PLT loads are proved, not replaced by magical direct calls. Required `OPENSSL_3.0.0` binding, resolved pointers, immutable code and ordinary page permissions are explicit post-initialization assumptions. No interposition, lazy binding, arbitrary ASLR or proof of how the loader produced the image is included.

Supplier writable image/padding values are not universally fixed to raw-file contents. Caller transport proves only the required current predicates and snapshots from actual prior states. Reusing the image variables for current memory is an explicit interface instantiation, not an assumption that mutable pages keep original initial values.

The separately accepted `CRYPTO_memcmp` architectural trace theorem is not a caller trace theorem and is not an elapsed-time guarantee. Cleanup establishes zeros in the designated region; it does not establish elimination of all copies or physical/microarchitectural remnants. This release establishes no C-language refinement, whole OpenSSL/glibc/library closure or complete dynamic-linker verification.

## Validation and independence

Historical fresh acceptance used offline read-only source reconstruction in new containers on the same Apple Silicon host and Docker service. Native ARM HOL checks the mathematical x86 model; GNU x86 extraction runs under emulation. Those runs are not native x86 execution, independent human review or external-organizational validation.

The release's package-consumption results and their precise host/filesystem/Docker/operator independence properties are recorded separately in `development/approved-plan/review-release-v1/consumer/results-v1.json` and the authoritative release index. Preparing this packet does not assert that a pending consumer run succeeded. Checking after a historical deadline must retain that original deadline guard and use a separately identified checking-only context with unchanged mathematical inputs and acceptance obligations.
